# Pork Ragù

## Ingredients

- pork, cut into chunks
- 2 teaspoons kosher salt
- 1 teaspoons black pepper
- 3 tablespoons olive oil
- 1 cups yellow onion, diced
- 0.5 cups carrot, diced
- 0.5 cups celery, diced
- 5 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 tablespoons tomato paste
- 1 cups dry red wine
- 28 ounces whole peeled tomatoes, hand-crushed
- 1 cups chicken or beef stock
- 2 bay leaves
- 1 fresh rosemary sprig
- 3 fresh thyme sprigs
- 1 pounds pappardelle or rigatoni
- 0.5 cups parmesan, grated, for serving

## Method

1. Season and sear: Pat pork chunks dry. Season all over with 2 teaspoons kosher salt and 1 teaspoons black pepper. Heat 3 tablespoons olive oil in a Dutch oven over medium-high until shimmering. Sear pork on all sides until deeply browned, about 15 minutes per side. Don't crowd — work in batches if needed. Transfer to a plate.
2. Build the soffritto: Reduce heat to medium. Add 1 cups yellow onion, diced, 0.5 cups carrot, diced, and 0.5 cups celery, diced to the pot. Cook, scraping up the fond, until softened and lightly golden, about 10 minutes . Add 5 garlic cloves, minced and cook 1 minute more.
3. Toast tomato paste: Stir in 2 tablespoons tomato paste and cook, stirring, until it darkens and smells caramelized, about 2 minutes .
4. Deglaze with wine: Pour in 1 cups dry red wine, scraping the bottom of the pot. Simmer until reduced by half, about 5 minutes .
5. Add tomatoes and braising liquid: Add 28 ounces whole peeled tomatoes, hand-crushed, 1 cups chicken or beef stock, 2 bay leaves, 1 fresh rosemary sprig, and 3 fresh thyme sprigs. Stir to combine. Return pork and any juices to the pot, nestling the meat into the sauce. Liquid should come about three-quarters up the meat — add a splash more stock if needed.
6. Braise low and slow: Bring to a simmer, then cover and transfer to a 300°F oven (or keep on stovetop at lowest simmer). Braise for 3 to 3.180 minutes , until the pork is fork-tender and pulls apart easily but still holds rough shape. Check at 2.5 hours and flip the meat.
7. Shred and finish sauce: Remove pork to a board. Discard bay leaves and herb stems. Skim excess fat from sauce surface. Using two forks, pull pork into rustic chunks (not fully shredded — leave some bite). Return meat to sauce. Taste and adjust salt. Simmer uncovered 10 minutes to thicken if needed.
8. Cook pasta and serve: Cook 1 pounds pappardelle or rigatoni in well-salted water until al dente. Drain, reserving a cup of pasta water. Toss pasta with ragù, adding splashes of pasta water to loosen. Serve topped with 0.5 cups parmesan, grated, for serving.

## Notes

- 2026-07-28 — Freezes beautifully — portion the finished ragù (without pasta) in 2-cup containers, freeze up to 3 months. Reheat gently with a splash of water or stock.
- 2026-07-28 — A parmesan rind tossed in during the braise adds great depth if you have one.
- 2026-07-28 — For a deeper flavor, braise a day ahead and reheat — it's better the next day.
